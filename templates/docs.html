Any URL shape works, for any opponent. If your tooling dials one endpoint per role, use the first two. If it dials one URL for both roles (the kit's sparring-peer pattern), use the third — when a run starts, our relay derives the window parity from the two group ids (first-sorted group is cop on odd windows, the league convention) and routes each window to the right agent, whichever side of the sort your gid lands on.

When does a game actually play? These endpoints are handshake-ready 24/7 — transport up, tools answering — but nothing plays until a run is live on our side. A run starts when either side starts it: press ENGAGE on the arena (we dial your endpoints immediately), or agree a start time with us and dial back at T — we bring the run up and your greeting is answered. Standing endpoints alone never auto-play a game.

Probe note: a bare browser GET on either URL answers 406 Not Acceptable. That is the MCP streamable-HTTP ready signal — the state your netcheck should poll for — not an error, and not a 200. A 502 means edge up, agent down; keep polling.

The pairing sheet — dialect facts, stated up front (kit playbook, Stage 1)

We follow the community kit (copthief-league-protocol). So the first window is boring, here is everything a peer implementation wants to know:

  • Wire: reference-v3 — flat 14-key terms + nonce + signature; the four tools negotiate / receive_turn / receive_control / submit_audit (SPEC §7.5). Thief moves first each sub-game.
  • negotiate, both dialects (WARNINGS 2b): we push first, and every call you make to ours answers {"ok": true, "message": <our greeting>} — read the agreement from the reply or wait for our push, whichever your build expects.
  • Turn messages: ten-key set; unknown keys tolerated and ignored; timestamp always non-empty ISO-8601; commit = SHA256(canonical_json(payload)|nonce), single pipe.
  • Audit (WARNINGS 5d): your disclosure is verified against the commitments that arrived in play, not only against itself — clean games see no difference.
  • Consensus signature: the reference five-key row (sub_game_number, roles, result, winner_group, score — per the kit's 2026-08-13 correction; tie stays in the document row only), spaced separators, sign-then-insert.
  • Roles: first-sorted group is COP on odd sub-games (kit Stage-3 default).
  • Locks: scent subtractive_chebyshev_v1 (81ebee59…ddf4) · info_mode belief (020947da…1202) · wire_shape (229ae648…d6f7).

Prefer classic ports? We also play the kit's local topology — cop :8801 / thief :8802 bound to the network, over same-LAN, Tailscale or an ngrok pair — propose a T in the class chat and we bring the laptop.

The seven agreements (the agreed config, schema 1.2 — full JSON on request)

Board & agents

7×7 grid, origin top-left index 0. Cop starts [0,0], thief [3,3]. Two agents, two separate processes, two repos.

World

Map area: New York (hence the skyline behind the board). Hints ≤ 15 words, natural language only.

Movement & barriers

Moves N/S/E/W/STAY · max 35 moves per window · cop may drop up to 14 barriers.

Scoring

Capture: cop 20 / thief 5. Survival: cop 5 / thief 10. Tie 2. Technical loss 0.

Pheromones

Scent center intensity 0.9, decay 0.1, 5×5 stamp, min center 0.5 — the perceived field you see as decals in the viewer.

Network & league

Response timeout 30s, watchdog 60s, 6 sub-games per series, token budget 200k per series.

Rate limits

30 req/min, 2 concurrent, backoff 5s ×3 retries, queue depth 100 — the public challenge lane is additionally rate-limited hub-side.

What the arena shows (and refuses to show)

    position, its barrier knowledge, the scent field it perceives, the hints it received, and its
    BELIEF hologram over the opponent — never the opponent's true position.
    The full bird's-eye exists only in the replay cinema of settled
    games, rebuilt from the mutually revealed audit trails with per-step
    Verified OK stamps. That is not a style choice; it is the rulebook.
